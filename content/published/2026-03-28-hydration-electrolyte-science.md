# STOP WASTING YOUR WORKOUT ON POOR HYDRATION

Hydration is not just about water. It is about electrolyte balance. Most people drink too much plain water during intense exercise. This dilutes the sodium levels in the blood. This condition is called hyponatremia. It causes muscle weakness and fatigue. It can be dangerous. You must replace the minerals you lose in sweat. Your sweat contains sodium and potassium and magnesium. These are the sparks that fire your nervous system.

![Hydration during workout](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1548690312-e3b507d17a4d?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

## THE SODIUM SIGNAL

Sodium is the most important electrolyte for muscle contraction. When you lose sodium, your muscles cannot fire at full capacity. You feel heavy and sluggish. Your heart rate increases to compensate for the lower blood volume. This makes your workout feel harder than it actually is. You must consume five hundred milligrams of sodium for every hour of heavy exercise. This maintains blood volume and blood pressure. This is the simplest way to sustain power output.

## MAGNESIUM AND RECOVERY

Magnesium is responsible for over three hundred biochemical reactions in the body. It is critical for muscle relaxation. If you are low on magnesium, you will experience cramping and poor sleep. Most soil is depleted of magnesium. This means most food is also depleted. Supplementation is often necessary for high performers. Magnesium glycinate is the best form for absorption and relaxation. Take it in the evening to improve sleep quality and muscle recovery.

## POTASSIUM FOR ENDURANCE

Potassium works with sodium to maintain fluid balance inside and outside of cells. It prevents the buildup of lactic acid. High potassium intake is linked to better cardiovascular health and lower blood pressure. Foods like avocados and spinach are excellent sources. You need about four thousand seven hundred milligrams of potassium per day. Most people consume less than half of that. This is a massive performance gap. You must bridge this gap to reach your potential.

![Electrolyte rich foods](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1512621776951-a57141f2eefd?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

## THE PERFORMANCE PROTOCOL

Start your day with sixteen ounces of water and a pinch of sea salt. This rehydrates you after the overnight fast. Drink sixteen ounces of water for every pound of body weight lost during exercise. Use an electrolyte powder that contains zero sugar. Sugar is not necessary for most workouts under ninety minutes. It only adds unnecessary calories. Focus on the minerals. Focus on the timing. Your performance is a chemical equation.

![Advanced nutrition science](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1543362906-acfc16c67564?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

## BEYOND THE BOTTLE

Hydration status affects your cognitive performance. Even a two percent drop in hydration leads to poor focus. You make mistakes in your training and in your work. Stay ahead of the thirst signal. If you feel thirsty, you are already dehydrated. Keep a bottle of electrolyte water with you at all times. This is the discipline of a high performer. Every detail matters when you are striving for excellence.
